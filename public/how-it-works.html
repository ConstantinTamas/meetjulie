How it works

Nine stages, and where you decide

Julie follows the stages a systematic review actually has. What follows is that work from your side: what she prepares, what comes back to you, what you decide, and how the review carries on from your decision.

EXAMPLE · A GUIDELINE UPDATEIt crosses this chain once. The F2F consensus meeting you already have in the calendar is one of the decisions on the way, and it arrives with the panel's reading material already prepared.

From your question to a search strategy

Julie helps turn your research questions into draft search strategies for PubMed, Embase and CENTRAL in the Cochrane Library. She asks you to clarify the scope and prepares a strategy for each database. You review and approve the final strategies, then run the searches and export the results.

The search settings and your approval stay with the strategy. If the question or strategy changes, the new version comes back to you for review.

Stage by stage

  1. 1. Research questions

    What happens
    You write them
    What you have afterwards
    the question set every later stage reads

  2. 2. Eligibility criteria

    What happens
    Julie drafts them from your questions, you settle the wording
    What you have afterwards
    the criteria every screening decision rests on

  3. 3. Search strategy preparation

    What happens
    Julie clarifies the scope with you and prepares a draft strategy for each database
    What you have afterwards
    draft strategies for PubMed, Embase and CENTRAL, with their search settings, ready for your review

  4. ↓ Approve, run and export searches

    What happens
    You approve the final strategies, run the searches in the databases and bring back the exports
    What you have afterwards
    the records to screen against your criteria

  5. 4. Title and abstract screening

    What happens
    each record assessed twice, you decide where they differ
    What you have afterwards
    the retained set, the upper half of the PRISMA flow, and the worklist of full texts to obtain

  6. ↓ Retrieving the full texts

    What happens
    You obtain what you can and say what you could not
    What you have afterwards
    a retrieval record with a stated reason for every report missing from it

  7. 5. Full-text eligibility check

    What happens
    Julie checks each full text against your questions. You can review and correct how a study relates to them
    What you have afterwards
    which studies carry evidence, and a recorded reason for each one set aside

  8. 6. Extraction and rating

    What happens
    Julie extracts and rates, each study assessed twice
    What you have afterwards
    study-level ratings, each traceable to the quote it rests on

  9. 7. Evidence brief for the panel

    What happens
    Julie writes it from the rated evidence
    What you have afterwards
    a brief the panel can read beforehand, and a dossier per question

  10. ↓ Consensus meeting

    What happens
    You and the panel decide, you record the outcome
    What you have afterwards
    the agreed recommendations and the record of what was decided

  11. 8. Linking evidence to recommendations

    What happens
    Julie links supporting and contradicting evidence to the wording the panel actually agreed, including amendments
    What you have afterwards
    the evidence behind each recommendation, each with a verified quote

  12. 9. Manuscript draft

    What happens
    Julie drafts, behind the decisions above
    What you have afterwards
    a drafted discussion, a record of coverage, and the author review still to do

No evidence, no rating

A study is rated only once it has been shown to yield usable evidence, so an unsuitable paper cannot carry a full evidence level through the rest of the review. What fell out, and why, is on the record, which is also what the PRISMA box for excluded reports needs.

Decisions along the way

  • 01

    Adjudicating the screening

    Every record is assessed twice against your criteria. Disagreements and uncertain cases come to you for a documented decision. Your screening decisions are recorded under your name.

  • 02

    Confirming the full texts you obtained

    Julie gives you the worklist of full texts to obtain, named article by article. You obtain what you can and tell her what you could not, and why. The evidence work starts from that confirmation, and your reasons become the review's account of the reports it could not retrieve.

  • 03

    The full-text eligibility check

    You review the full-text eligibility results, with a recorded reason for each study set aside. The record shows which studies contribute evidence to your review and which need your attention.

  • 04

    The consensus meeting and its record

    Julie prepares what the panel reads beforehand: a brief written to be shared, with a dossier behind it for each question. After the meeting you record what was decided for each recommendation, accepted, amended, rejected or deferred, and give the final wording of anything amended. The evidence work behind the recommendations waits for that record.

When approval is needed

  • 05

    Approving what a paid stage will cost

    No stage that costs money starts on its own. Julie estimates it first, on your own corpus and at no cost, and waits for the ceiling you set and your approval. Declining costs nothing and breaks nothing. A review has several paid stages, so this decision comes back more than once.

  • 06

    Releasing material that is still incomplete

    If a question failed or was never processed, Julie will not hand you a document that reads as finished. She shows you what is missing and in what state, because a question that found no evidence is a finding and one whose processing broke is not. Releasing it anyway is yours to decide, and what goes out is marked partial.

So the claim is not that the chain runs end to end. It is this: the automated stages run end to end, and where a decision is yours, a named person makes it and that decision leaves a trace.
