Who Julie is for

EXAMPLE · A GUIDELINE UPDATEThe update belongs to a panel and its secretariat, and the person running it is also, this month, a systematic review author working without a second screener.

  • Systematic review authors

    You are running the review with fewer hands than the method assumes, and the second screener is the first thing to go. Julie assesses every record twice and brings you the ones the two assessments could not settle, so your methods section can state dual screening with human adjudication.

    What stays yours: approving the criteria and search strategies, running the searches, and every call on a record the assessments disagreed about.

  • Guideline panels and their secretariat

    You have to put something in front of a panel that can be voted on, and afterwards show that the vote was about that material. Julie prepares a dossier per question and a brief to circulate beforehand.

    What you do: record what the panel decided, recommendation by recommendation, with the final wording of anything it amended. The evidence work behind the recommendations waits for that record.

  • HTA and evidence synthesis units

    You run several reviews at once, and the real risk is one that stalls where nobody can say what state it is in. Julie tells you which stage each review is in, what is missing, and which decision is waiting on a person.

    What you get: no stage spends anything without an estimate you have seen and a ceiling you set, and each run reports what it spent.

  • Methods editors and peer reviewers

    You have to decide whether a submitted review's method is defensible. What you are reading is not prose written afterwards from memory: the methods report is produced from the run's own record, and where it cannot supply something the checklist asks for, it names that item instead of reading as complete.

If you are running a systematic review, sitting on a guideline panel, or working in an evidence synthesis unit, tell us what you are working on.
