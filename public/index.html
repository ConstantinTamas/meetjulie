The ochre lines are the human gates. You decide there, and the decision is recorded under your name.

How it works

Nine stages, and where you decide

Julie follows the stages a systematic review actually has. What follows is that work from your side: what she prepares, what comes back to you, what you decide, and how the review carries on from your decision.

EXAMPLE · A GUIDELINE UPDATEIt crosses this chain once. The F2F consensus meeting you already have in the calendar is one of the decisions on the way, and it arrives with the panel's reading material already prepared.

The preparation starts with your question: Julie helps draft search strategies for PubMed, Embase and CENTRAL in the Cochrane Library. You review and approve them before running the searches. Each later decision stays with a named person, with a record of what was agreed.

Scope

Where Julie belongs, and where she does not

EXAMPLE · A GUIDELINE UPDATENothing in it happens at a bedside. It happens at a desk, months before anyone acts on it, and it has to hold up when a methods editor reads it. The distance is in time, not in purpose: there are patients behind every piece of evidence in it, and the recommendation is written for them.

Julie is

  • A research instrument for evidence synthesis: systematic reviews, guideline development, evidence summaries and health technology assessment.
  • Built for work that will be read by a methods editor, a peer reviewer or a guideline panel, and has to survive that reading.
  • A workbench with named human gates, where the researcher decides and the machine does the volume work in between.

Julie is not

  • Not a clinical decision support tool. She answers nothing at the bedside, gives no treatment advice, and has no place in a consultation.
  • Not a point-of-care reference. She is not an alternative to the summaries clinicians use to look something up during the working day.
  • Database searches stay in your hands. Julie helps prepare the strategies for PubMed, Embase and CENTRAL. You approve them, run the searches and bring back the results.
  • Not an autopilot. She does not decide what goes into your review, and she does not sign anything on your behalf.

The distinction matters because the two jobs have opposite failure modes. A point-of-care tool has to be fast and confident. An evidence synthesis instrument has to be slow enough to show its work, and honest enough to stop when it does not know something. None of that is academic caution. A recommendation that is wrong will be wrong in someone's treatment, and by then nobody is rereading the methods section.

Who Julie is for→

Capabilities

What you get

  • Every record is assessed twice against your criteria: where the two assessments agree the decision stands, and where they differ you decide.

  • Dual screening with human adjudication, stated in your methods section, even when there is no second person in the building.

  • Evidence rated by Oxford CEBM or GRADE as published, each rating traceable to the sentence it rests on.

  • Every human decision under the name of the person who made it, so the review can be reported and repeated by someone who was not there.
